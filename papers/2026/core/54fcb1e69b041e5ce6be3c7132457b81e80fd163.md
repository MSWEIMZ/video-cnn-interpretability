## abstract

The selective nature of human visual attention is fundamentally altered under stereoscopic three-dimensional (3D) viewing. Binocular disparity introduces depth pop-out, vergence-driven focus, and binocular comfort zones that significantly reshape the spatial distribution of viewer fixations in ways that no monocular saliency model can capture. In this paper we propose BCAT-Net (Binocular Cross-Attention Transformer Network), a novel end-to-end deep learning framework for visual saliency prediction in stereoscopic video. BCAT-Net integrates: (i) a shared-weight ResNet-50 encoder for view-invariant feature extraction; (ii) a Binocular Attention Module (BAM) that performs explicit cross-attention between left and right feature maps to model inter-view disparity dependencies; (iii) a Depth-Contrast Feature Extractor (DCFE) that captures the depth pop-out effect via local disparity contrast; (iv) a ConvLSTM temporal module for video-coherent prediction with an optical-flow warping consistency loss; and (v) a physiologically grounded comfort-zone regularisation term that penalises saliency predictions in visually uncomfortable high-disparity regions. Extensive experiments on four benchmarks-NJU2000, NLPR, DViSal (NeurIPS 2023), and KITTI-Stereo 2015-demonstrate that BCAT-Net achieves state-of-the-art performance with AUC-J 0.891, NSS 2.57, and CC 0.79, surpassing all prior methods including Deep3DSaliency and the recent Cheng-VSPS (2024). A formal eye-tracking study with 24 participants under actual stereoscopic viewing conditions confirms a 14.2% improvement in predicted-vs.-actual fixation agreement, with the largest gains in high-disparity scenes where binocular attention deviates most strongly from monocular predictions. BCAT-Net runs at 28.7 fps on a single GPU and establishes a new state of the art in stereoscopic visual attention modelling.
