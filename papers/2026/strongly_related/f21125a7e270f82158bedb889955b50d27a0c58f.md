## abstract

The speed at which online multimedia data is soaring has brought into question the issues of digital forgery especially on online video data whereas data tampering may affect the authenticity of evidence, security and trust. Conventional deep learning methods are highly accurate in the task of forgery detection but are not particularly interpretable and require substantial training data. To resolve this, we suggest a Supervised Learning and Explainable AI (SLT-XAI) framework that is applied to explainable feature selection using SHAP and LIME, with the robust video forgery detection after being trained on a deep learning model. Transformed in Image videos, feature representations are the results of video frames that are first pre-processed. SHAP and LIME are then used to estimate the feature importance and eradicate redundant/noise attributes so to achieve efficiency and interpretability in model. The extracted features are forwarded to a deep learning classifier (CNN-LSTM) to exploit spatial as well as temporal inconsistencies in the video. Unlike the already available heuristic-optimization methods, the SLT-XAI model enhances accuracy and transparency since forensic practitioners have a better idea of the role of the features in the process of tampering decisions. To validate the DFD (Deep-Fake Detection), it was tried to use the VTD (Video Tampering Dataset). Face manipulation forgeries are captured by FD, and general copy-move tampering of videos by VTD. The proposed SLT-XAI framework shows robustness to various forgery types and achieves a better level of performance $(94-96 \%)$ and explainability (face-level and object-level level). This paper underlines the possibility of using explainable AI and deep learning in the field of trustworthy digital forensics.
