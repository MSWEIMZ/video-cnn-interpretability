## abstract

A The fast-growing development of deepfake technology poses serious threats to digital trust, media integrity, and information authenticity. Current detection methodologies may be effective but often struggle with issues of generalization, computational expense, and a lack of interpretability. a novel framework, "Countering Deepfakes: A Transformer-Informed and Explainable Framework, for Robust Video Integrity Verification," which adopts a hybrid convolutional neural network (CNN) and transformer-based architectures to model local spatial aspects and long-range temporal aspects of video information. Additionally, multimodal analysis, through visual and audio consistency checks, significantly contributes to detection reliability. To meet the interpretability challenges, Here applies a gradient-based interpretability methodology to identify facial manipulation regions for forensic overlay. Empirical evaluation of our framework on established benchmark deepfake datasets shows promise for comparably higher accuracy, cross-data-set generalization, and adversarial robustness compared to prior CNN–RNN-based deepfake methodologies. This reduced a computational demands and enabled ~real-time™ deployment, in a version suitable for digital media forensics, social media engagements, and digital information cybersecurity. Our work extends the state of the art in video integrity detection by offering a robust, interpretable, and scalable solution to counter deepfakes in a rapidly changing digital environment.
