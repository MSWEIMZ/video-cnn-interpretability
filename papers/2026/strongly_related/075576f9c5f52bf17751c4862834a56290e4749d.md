## abstract

Understanding sign language requires careful consideration of the relationships between local features, such as the face and hands, and global features of the whole body. Most previous studies, however, did not fully utilize these relationships, resulting in inefficient training. To address this issue, we propose a model that explicitly captures the relationships between local and global information for continuous sign language recognition (CSLR). Our method consists of two phases: extracting the face and hand regions from a whole body video and learning the connections between the local and global features using a convolutional neural network. In the first phase, we employ the MediaPipe framework to detect the face and hand regions from the input video. Subsequently, the proposed relational convolution module (RCM) captures spatiotemporal relationships between local and global features by applying a convolution operation across adjacent frames. This method enables our model to effectively analyze the relationships between the local and global characteristics in CSLR. Our model achieved Word Error Rates (WER) of 19.4%, 20.2%, and 26.2% on PHOENIX2014, PHOENIX2014-T, and CSL-Daily datasets, respectively. These results are comparable to or better than those of existing methods on PHOENIX2014, PHOENIX2014-T, and CSL-Daily datasets.
