## abstract

Ejection fraction (EF) is a key indicator of left ventricular function, commonly measured with echocardiogram videos. Recent transformer-based models offer an annotation-efficient path for directly predicting EF, end-diastolic volume (EDV), and end-systolic volume (ESV). In this study, we examine how different frame embedding strategies affect performance and interpretability in this setting. We compare a simple linear projection, an ImageNet-pretrained ResNet-50, and selfsupervised contrastive encoders using temporally adjacent or within-video positive pairs. Our analysis includes both quantitative regression results and visualizations of learned temporal structure. While contrastive embeddings reveal clear alignment with cardiac phases, they do not improve regression accuracy. The linear embedding consistently achieves the best performance across settings. These findings suggest that preserving fine-grained frame-level detail may be more effective for beat-wise volume prediction than introducing strong temporal abstraction through contrastive objectives.
