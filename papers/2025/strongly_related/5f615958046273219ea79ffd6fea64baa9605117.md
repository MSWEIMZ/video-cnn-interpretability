## abstract

Deep learning has achieved impressive performance in image-based tasks across a wide range of domains, including medical diagnostics, agricultural monitoring, and surveillance systems. Despite this success, the complex nature of deep neural networks poses significant challenges to their transparency, reliability, and widespread adoption; particularly in high-stakes applications. Explainable AI (XAI) techniques have become as a critical solution to bridge this gap by making model predictions more understandable and trustworthy. This review paper focuses on three widely applied explainability AI methods: Gradient-weighted Class Activation Mapping (Grad-CAM), Local Interpretable Model-agnostic Explanations (LIME) and SHapley Additive exPlanations (SHAP). We examine these methods on the basis of interpretability, visual explanation quality, computational complexity, and domain suitability. While studying the recent researches which involves histological imaging, biological microscopy and surveillance footage, the paper compares how each method performs across various imaging contexts. The research analysis highlights the balance that must be maintained between being independent of specific models, achieving accurate localization, and ensuring easy integration into real-world systems. By synthesizing current findings, we offer insights into the strengths and limitations of each technique and suggest directions for future research focused on building more robust, interpretable, and domain-adaptive deep learning systems suitable for critical image analysis tasks.
