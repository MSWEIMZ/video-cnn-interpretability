## abstract

Video-based violence detection faces the challenge of balancing accuracy, computational efficiency, and generalization for practical deployment. This study begins with a comparative analysis of ConvGRU versus ConvLSTM on the ResNet50 architecture, where ConvGRU demonstrates superior performance (87 % vs 72 % accuracy with SCan attention) with lower parameters, making it an optimal choice for temporal modeling. Next, a comprehensive evaluation of the attention mechanisms in ResNet50-ConvGRU is conducted, comparing Temporal Excitation and Attention (TEA), Spatial and Channel Attention (SCan), Convolutional Block Attention Module (CBAM), and their combinations. The experiment performed on Violence dataset, namely Automatic Violence Dataset (AVD) and Real-Life Video Surveillance (RLVS). Through rigorous ablation study and hyperparameter optimization (learning rate 1e-7, dropout 0.2, SCan ratio 2, Adam optimizer), the combined spatial-temporal attention (SCan+TEA) achieves 97.3% accuracy on AVD with minimal computational overhead, only 49.15 Giga Multiply-Add Operations per Second (GMACs) compared to the baseline of 49 GMACs, indicating efficient feature sharing with a combined overhead of 0.02 GMACs. SCan+TEA requires $2 \times$ less computation (49.15 vs 98.15 GMACs) and inference is 27 % faster than CBAM + TEA with identical accuracy, accompanied by superior training stability (negative train-validation gap $-0.22 \%$, zero degradation). In contrast, CBAM+TEA suffers from severe overfitting (training accuracy $\mathbf{1 0 0 \%}$ vs validation $\mathbf{9 7. 8 3 \%}$). The results provide practical guidance in selecting spatiotemporal models and attention mechanisms for movement patterns such as violence detection that tend to be aggressive, sudden, and repetitive.
