## abstract

Human action recognition (HAR) using deep learning approaches has significantly improved our lives, including elderly care, child monitoring, and sports analytics, particularly where human surveillance is limited due to ample spatial coverage and human resource constraints. HAR using still images has unique challenges compared to video-based approaches. Without motion cues or temporal context, distinguishing between visually similar actions in single frames becomes very difficult, increasing reliance on subtle visual features like body posture, human-object interaction, and background context. Apart from these challenges, still-image HAR provides computational efficiency, reduced storage, and practical deployment advantages. Our literature review reveals substantial progress in still-image HAR through semantic features, part-based approaches, CNN extractors, attention mechanisms, and transfer learning. Despite these advances, researchers have devoted limited attention to ensemble methodologies that integrate explainable AI for enhanced transparency and performance, a critical oversight for practical deployment scenarios. We address these challenges using transfer learning with four pre-trained CNN architectures (InceptionResNetV2, VGG19, EfficientNetB3, Xception) on the Data Sprint 76 dataset containing 15 action classes. We implemented two ensemble strategies: weighted averaging and stacking with logistic regression meta-learner. Our stacking ensemble achieved 97% validation accuracy with 0.998 micro-average ROC-AUC, while weighted averaging reached 95% accuracy with 0.997 ROC-AUC, significantly outperforming individual models (best: EfficientNetB3 at 81.8%). To enhance interpretability, we integrated Grad-CAM for individual models and Grad-CAM++ for ensemble models, enabling transparent visualization of decision-making processes. XAI visualizations confirmed focus on semantically relevant regions: hand positions for device interactions, body posture for physical activities, and contextual objects. To the best of our knowledge, this work presents the first integration of dual ensemble strategies (weighted averaging and meta-learning) with comprehensive XAI visualization (Grad-CAM and Grad-CAM++) for still-image HAR on the Data Sprint 76 dataset. Unlike existing approaches that sacrifice either performance for explainability or vice versa, our novel framework uniquely unifies superior classification performance with interpretability, addressing a critical need for transparent AI systems in practical deployment scenarios.
