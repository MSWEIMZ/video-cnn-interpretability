## abstract

Unstructured environments, shifting motion patterns, and a lack of labelled anomalous data make surveillance-based video anomaly detection difficult, especially in complex crime scenes. This paper presents a comparative analysis for assessing a number of sophisticated deep learning paradigms, such as Vision Transformers (TimeSformer, ViViT), Spatio-Temporal Graph Neural Networks (ST-GNNs), Memory-Augmented Networks and Diffusion Models on the UCF-Crime dataset. Unlike earlier research that was exclusively concerned with the detection accuracy, our research prioritizes interpretability using a novel Explainability-Integrated Benchmarking Protocol (EIBP). The EIBP assesses both prediction performance (using accuracy, AUC and F1-score) and model transparency (using Grad-CAM, LIME, and SHAP). Explainability is quantified through an Explainability Fidelity Index (EFI) that measures the similarity between the model’s output and its corresponding explanations. The results illustrate that hybrid architectures that combine spatio-temporal reasoning with integrated explainable analysis achieve an accuracy of approximately 95% and demonstrate strong potential to create transparent, reliable and scalable crime video anomaly detection systems suitable for real-world forensic applications.
