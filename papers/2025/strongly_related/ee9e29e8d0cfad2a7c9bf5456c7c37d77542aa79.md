## abstract

Transfer learning from large-scale pretrained models has become essential for achieving strong performance in specialized domains, yet the process remains resource-intensive in terms of storage, computation, and processing time. This paper investigates whether aggressive video dataset compression can maintain effective transfer learning performance for action recognition tasks. Several varying parameters, such as frame rate, spatial resolution and Constant Rate Factor (CRF), are used to analyze the effect of reduced data on the model fine-tuning outcomes. For this purpose, the R(2+1)D architecture on the UCF101 dataset is used. The obtained results demonstrate that significant computational and storage savings are achievable in transfer learning without proportional accuracy costs, and the possibility of providing an automated platform to facilitate compression experimentation is shown to be valuable for resource-constrained scenarios.
